## Findings & recommendation

**Verdict: do NOT flip `AEGIS_INTENT_CLASSIFIER='heuristic'` as currently tuned.** The scorer collapses almost everything to Haiku and the safety nudge never fires.

1. **The score is effectively bimodal: 0.30 or 0.60, nothing between.** All 18 Haiku-routed queries scored *exactly* 0.30 (base only — **no nudge ever fired**). The 5 Sonnet queries scored 0.60 (multiReg 0.30 + cross 0.25 − clamp/overlap). There is no gradient; the 0.50 threshold isn't really doing any work.

2. **The anchorless nudge is dead for the queries it was designed to catch.** It requires `regCount === 0` **and** `len > 140`. The deceptively-hard Band-D questions are short (17–40 chars) so the `len > 140` gate blocks them; Band A/E questions name a regulation so `regCount > 0` blocks them. Net: the one signal meant to escalate "short, anchorless, hard" questions fired **zero times** — visible as the all-zero `anchorless` column in Step 2. This is the root cause.

3. **Under-powering risk — Haiku-routed queries where Sonnet is materially deeper:**
   - **Severe (Haiku returned 0 KB citations — ungrounded, yet verify passed):** "What logging do I need?" (Haiku 0 cit vs Sonnet 11), "What's the penalty for non-compliance?" (0 vs 9), "Is my AI system high-risk?" (0 vs 6), "What is DORA?" (0 vs 8).
   - **Notable (materially fewer citations):** "Do I need a DPIA for my AI chatbot?" (3 vs 10), "Is my chatbot high-risk?" (2 vs 7), "What are the main obligations under the EU AI Act?" (Band E, 7 vs 14), "Explain automated decision-making rules." (5 vs 8).
   - **Where Haiku held up / beat Sonnet:** "Who's liable if the model gets it wrong?" (11 vs 6), "Can I use AI for credit scoring?" (9 vs 8), "Do I need a DPIA?" (tie 2/2). Haiku is not uniformly worse — but the heuristic can't tell these apart from the severe cases (all score 0.30).

4. **Verify blind spot (orthogonal to 3.4, but it amplifies the risk):** Haiku sometimes answered with **zero citations and still passed verify** — `citation_coverage` only fails on an *uncited article reference*; a generic, ungrounded answer with no article refs passes vacuously. So under-powering is not caught downstream.

5. **A genuinely deep multi-part question slipped to Haiku:** Band C "Walk me through everything a deployer must do …" scored 0.30 (len 160, single regulation) → Haiku gave the same 9 citations but ~half the depth of Sonnet (3,937 vs 6,700 chars).

**Recommended retune (separate code step — NOT done here):** relax/remove the `len > 140` gate on the anchorless nudge; add question-pattern signals that correlate with deep regulatory reasoning ("do I need", "is my … high-risk", "who is liable", "penalty", "can I use"); consider raising the base or lowering the threshold. Then re-run this eval. Until then the code default `'haiku'` should stay.

> The GDPR establishes a tiered framework restricting when personal data may be sent to third countries or international organisations [R-GDPR-044]. Three mechanisms are available, applied in order of preference:
> - **Adequacy Decision (Art. 45):** The European Commission has formally determined that the destination country offers an equivalent level of protection — no further safeguards are needed [R-GDPR-044].
> - **Appropriate Safeguards (Art. 46):** Where no adequacy decision exists, transfers may proceed on the basis of Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs), approved codes of conduct, or approved certification mechanisms [R-GDPR-044].
> - **Specific Derogations (Art. 49):** As a last resort, transfers may rely on explicit informed consent, contractual necessity, important public interest, legal claims, vital interests, or transfer from a public register [R-GDPR-044].
